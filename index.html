00 — Durex Invisible

Zero gravity.
Zero worries.

So thin it floats. Durex calls Invisible the thinnest condom it has ever made.1

And strong enough to hold about forty litres of air — more than twice what the international standard asks for.2

01 — Rip

Tear here.
Gently.

It opens along the jagged edge, with a little drama and no struggle. Go on — it has been waiting.

Rrrip.

02 — Roll

Thin enough
to forget.

Out of the packet it is a ring. Unrolled, it is 180 millimetres of very nearly nothing.3

Squeak.

03 — Pump

Go on,
blow it up.

Hold the button — or the space bar — and pump. Scrolling fills it too. The gauge reads what the shape holds.

0.0 litres

18 litres. What the international standard asks a condom to hold.2

40 litres. What Durex says a typical one holds without bursting.2 Still holding.

Pfff.

04 — Let go

Now,
let go.

Every balloon’s favourite trick. Or tie it, and keep it.

Pfffrrrrt!

05 — Tested

Tested,
tested, tested.

Every Durex condom is tested electronically. Samples from every batch are blown up with air, and filled with water and hung for a minute to show any leak — about two million water tests a month.4

If one sample fails, the whole batch — up to 432,000 condoms — doesn’t leave the factory.5

Slosh.

06 — Play safe

Play safe

  1. “Durex’s thinnest condom ever” — Durex India, Invisible 003.
  2. About 40 litres, against a standard of 18 — Durex Nigeria, quality and Durex UK, quality tests; ISO 4074’s minimum burst volume — UNFPA / WHO specification.
  3. 180 mm long, 54 mm nominal width — a retail listing, WorldCondoms, Durex Invisible.
  4. Electronic testing, the air and water tests, about two million water tests a month — Durex UK, quality tests.
  5. A batch of up to 432,000 held back for one failed sample — Durex Nigeria, quality.
  6. World Health Organization, fact sheet: Sexually transmitted infections.
